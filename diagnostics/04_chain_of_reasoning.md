# Diagnostic 4 — From assertion to chain

**Module:** 专题 4 · **Items:** 4 multiple-choice + 1 written · **Time:** 10 minutes
**Targets:** the #2 mistake in the corpus — 207 examiner statements, 54 reports, all
five boards, 2015–2025.

This quiz is topic-agnostic on purpose. The error is not "not knowing macro"; it is
stopping an explanation one link early, and it recurs in every topic. AP Chief Reader
reports describe the same failure as *asserting* rather than explaining.

**The standard used throughout:** an explanation is complete when it runs
**trigger → mechanism → effect on the variable the question actually named**, with no
step the reader has to supply themselves.

---

### Q1
Question: *"Explain how a rise in interest rates may reduce inflation."*
Which answer is complete?

- A. "Higher interest rates reduce inflation because they reduce aggregate demand."
- B. "Higher interest rates reduce inflation. This is contractionary monetary policy."
- **C. "Higher interest rates raise the cost of borrowing and the return on saving, so households and firms cut consumption and investment. Aggregate demand falls, so the price level rises more slowly."** ✅
- D. "Higher interest rates reduce inflation, as shown by the fall in the AD curve on the diagram."

**Correct: C.** Three links, ending on the price level — the variable the question
named.

- *A* — **one link short.** *Why* does a rise in rates reduce demand? This is the single
  most common shape of the error.
- *B* — names the policy instead of explaining it. Labelling is not a mechanism.
- *C* — note it ends on *inflation*, not on "AD falls". Stopping at AD leaves the last
  step to the examiner.
- *D* — **describes the diagram rather than explaining it.** Separately reported 8 times
  across CAIE and AQA. A diagram supports an explanation; it cannot be one.

---

### Q2
Which sentence is an **assertion** rather than an explanation?

- **A. "A minimum wage will cause unemployment."** ✅
- B. "A minimum wage set above equilibrium means firms demand less labour while more workers supply it, creating a surplus of labour — unemployment."
- C. "A minimum wage raises labour costs, so firms facing fixed budgets hire fewer workers."
- D. "Because the minimum wage is a price floor, it can only bind if set above the market wage."

**Correct: A.** A claim with no supporting mechanism, however true it may be.

- *B, C* — both supply a mechanism. *D* supplies a condition, which is also reasoning.
- Picking B, C or D indicates the difference between a claim and an explanation is not
  yet visible — the same blind spot as Q4 in the elasticity diagnostic.

### Q3
Question: *"Explain how a depreciation of the currency may worsen a country's terms of
trade."* A student writes: *"A depreciation makes exports cheaper and imports dearer, so
the terms of trade worsen."* What is the best assessment?

- **A. Nearly complete: it needs one more link stating that export prices fall relative to import prices, so the ratio of export to import prices falls** ✅
- B. Complete as written
- C. Wrong: a depreciation improves the terms of trade
- D. Incomplete: it should also discuss the effect on the trade balance

**Correct: A.** The student has the two price movements but never connects them to the
*ratio*, which is what the terms of trade are.

- *B* — accepts a near-miss. The examiner cannot award the link that was not written.
- *C* — factually wrong.
- *D* — **adds material the question did not ask for.** The question named the terms of
  trade. Discussing the trade balance is the irrelevance error from 专题 5, and here it
  also risks re-merging two concepts 专题 1 works to separate.

### Q4
A question asks you to *"explain one reason"* for something and is worth 4 marks. What
does that mark allocation usually signal?

- **A. One reason developed through several linked steps, not several reasons listed** ✅
- B. Four separate reasons, one per mark
- C. One reason plus an evaluation of it
- D. One reason plus a diagram, with the diagram carrying most of the marks

**Correct: A.** Marks on an "explain one reason" question buy *depth*, not breadth.

- *B* — **the most expensive misreading available**, and it directly contradicts the
  command word.
- *C* — evaluation is not being asked for; unrequested evaluation earns nothing here.
- *D* — a diagram supports, it does not substitute.

### Q5 — Written (self-marked)

> *"Explain how an increase in government spending may increase real GDP."*
> Write your answer in 3–5 sentences.

Mark it yourself against this rubric. Award one point per row, honestly.

| | Check | Point |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Did you name the **trigger** precisely (a rise in G as a component of AD)? | ☐ |
| 2 | Did you give a **mechanism** — how the spending becomes someone's income, and what they do with it? | ☐ |
| 3 | Did you reach **real GDP** specifically, rather than stopping at "AD rises"? | ☐ |
| 4 | Is there any step where a reader must ask **"but why does that follow?"** — if so, lose this point | ☐ |
| 5 | Did you avoid adding **unasked material** (evaluation, crowding out, alternative policies)? | ☐ |

A model chain: *Government spending is a component of aggregate demand, so a rise in G
raises AD directly. That spending becomes income for firms and households, who spend a
proportion of it again, so the total rise in demand exceeds the initial injection.
Provided there is spare capacity, firms respond to higher demand by raising output, so
real GDP rises.*

Note where it ends: **real GDP**, the variable the question named. Note also what it
does not contain — no evaluation, no crowding out, no "however". Those belong in a
question that asks for them.
