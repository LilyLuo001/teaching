# Diagnostic 1 — Terms of trade: price or value?

**Module:** 专题 1 · **Items:** 5 · **Time:** 6 minutes
**Targets:** the single most persistent named confusion in the corpus — 33 examiner
statements across 15 reports, 2015–2025, CAIE/OCR/Edexcel.

The whole quiz probes one substitution: reasoning about the **value** of exports and
imports (revenue, spending, the trade balance) when the terms of trade depend only on
their **prices**. Examiners repeatedly note that candidates write the formula correctly
and *then* discuss the wrong quantity, so an item that only asks for the formula would
detect nothing. None of these items asks for the formula.

---

### Q1
A country's export prices rise by 10% over a year. Its import prices rise by 4% over
the same year. What has happened to its terms of trade?

- **A. Improved** ✅
- B. Worsened
- C. Cannot be determined without knowing export and import volumes
- D. Unchanged, because export and import prices both rose

**Correct: A.** The index moves to roughly 110/104 × 100 ≈ 105.8. Export prices rose
faster than import prices, so each unit of exports now buys more imports.

- *B* — direction reversed; check which price index is the numerator.
- *C* — **the core misconception.** Volumes are irrelevant to the terms of trade. Asking
  for them is the value-thinking error examiners report.
- *D* — treats "both rose" as cancelling; it is the *relative* rate that matters.

---

### Q2
A country's export prices are unchanged. Its import prices fall by 8%. Import volumes
rise so much that the country's **total spending on imports rises**. What has happened
to the terms of trade?

- **A. Improved** ✅
- B. Worsened, because total import spending rose
- C. Unchanged, because export prices did not move
- D. Cannot be determined without knowing what happened to export volumes

**Correct: A.** Import prices fell while export prices held, so the ratio rises. Total
import spending is a *value*, and values have no bearing on the terms of trade.

- *B* — **the documented error, in its purest form.** This is exactly what CAIE
  describes as discussing "the value rather than the prices of exports and imports"
  (Mar 2025). The item is built so that value and price point in opposite directions.
- *C* — misses that the ratio moves if *either* index moves.
- *D* — value thinking again, via volumes.

---

### Q3
Which one of the following would change a country's **balance of trade** but leave its
**terms of trade** unchanged?

- **A. A rise in the volume of exports sold, at unchanged export and import prices** ✅
- B. A rise in export prices, with import prices unchanged
- C. A fall in import prices, with export prices unchanged
- D. A rise in export prices and import prices in different proportions

**Correct: A.** Volume changes move the trade balance; only price changes move the
terms of trade. This item exists to force the two concepts apart.

- *B, C, D* — all move the price ratio, so all change the terms of trade. Choosing any
  of them means the two concepts are still fused.

---

### Q4
A country's terms of trade index, with a base year of 100, is now 96. Which statement
is correct?

- **A. The terms of trade have worsened: a given volume of exports now buys fewer imports** ✅
- B. The country is running a trade deficit
- C. The country's exports have fallen by 4%
- D. The country's import volumes have risen by 4%

**Correct: A.** The index expresses purchasing power of exports over imports, nothing
more.

- *B* — **the headline confusion**: reading a terms-of-trade index as a statement about
  the balance of trade or payments. Explicitly named in CAIE reports in 2016, 2022,
  2023 and 2025. A country can have a deficit with improving terms of trade, and a
  surplus with worsening ones.
- *C* — reads the index as an export volume or revenue figure.
- *D* — reads it as a volume figure.

---

### Q5
A country's currency depreciates. Assume firms hold their prices constant **in their
own domestic currency**. Which is most likely?

- **A. The terms of trade worsen, while the balance of trade may improve** ✅
- B. Both the terms of trade and the balance of trade worsen
- C. Both improve, because exports become more competitive
- D. The terms of trade improve, because exports become more competitive

**Correct: A.** Export prices fall in foreign-currency terms while import prices rise in
domestic-currency terms, so the ratio worsens. Improved competitiveness may raise
export volumes and improve the trade balance. **The two move in opposite directions.**

- *B* — misses that a worse price ratio is often the mechanism *through which* the trade
  balance improves.
- *C* and *D* — **treat "more competitive" as "better terms of trade".** Competitiveness
  is about price *level* relative to rivals; the terms of trade are a price *ratio*
  between a country's own exports and imports. Note that the pair
  "competitiveness vs contestability" is separately one of the most-cited named
  confusions in the corpus (3 mentions, Edexcel) — this family of terms is unstable for
  students generally.
