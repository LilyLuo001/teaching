# Diagnostic 2 — The elasticity family

**Module:** 专题 2 · **Items:** 5 · **Time:** 7 minutes
**Targets:** the highest-scoring topic in the corpus — 136 examiner statements. Within
it: concept confusion (39), assertion without a chain (32), answering beyond the
question (11).

Elasticity is where students are most likely to have memorised something correct and
still lose the marks. These items deliberately avoid rewarding recall of a formula.

---

### Q1
The cross elasticity of demand between good A and good B is measured as:

- **A. % change in quantity demanded of A ÷ % change in the price of B** ✅
- B. % change in the price of A ÷ % change in the price of B
- C. change in quantity demanded of A ÷ change in the price of B
- D. % change in quantity demanded of A ÷ % change in quantity demanded of B

**Correct: A.**

- *B* — **the documented inversion.** CAIE (Jun 2023) reports candidates putting the
  percentage change in the price of one good over the percentage change in the price of
  the other. Every elasticity has a *quantity response* on top and a *driver* below;
  price-over-price has no response variable at all.
- *C* — **omits "percentage".** Also explicitly reported in the same series. Without
  percentages the measure is unit-dependent and meaningless.
- *D* — quantity-over-quantity; again no driver.

### Q2
A good has a price elasticity of demand of −0.4. The government imposes a specific tax
on it. Which is most likely?

- **A. Most of the tax is passed to consumers, and government revenue is relatively secure** ✅
- B. Most of the tax is absorbed by producers, because demand is inelastic
- C. The tax will raise no revenue, because quantity will not fall
- D. Consumers and producers share the burden equally, because elasticity is less than 1

**Correct: A.** With inelastic demand, quantity is unresponsive, so the burden falls
mainly on consumers and the revenue base is stable.

- *B* — direction reversed. Inelastic *demand* shifts the burden **onto** consumers.
- *C* — confuses "quantity falls little" with "quantity does not fall".
- *D* — incidence depends on the *relative* elasticities of demand and supply, not on
  whether a number is below 1. This is the cross-chapter link students miss.

### Q3
A question asks: *"Explain how the price elasticity of demand for a firm's product
affects its pricing decision."* A student writes an accurate account of PED, YED and
XED, defining and giving a formula for each. What is the most likely outcome?

- **A. Marks are lost: the material on YED and XED cannot be credited, and time was spent for nothing** ✅
- B. Full marks, since everything written is accurate
- C. Full marks, because showing wider knowledge is always rewarded
- D. Marks are lost only if the PED section is also weak

**Correct: A.** Accuracy is not the criterion; relevance to the question set is. CAIE
(Nov 2022) records candidates writing about cross elasticity "even though this was not
required in the question", and separately warns that unrequested discussion "is
irrelevant and will not be rewarded."

- *B, C* — the "more is safer" belief. It is the single most expensive time error in the
  exam, and it is wrong on every board.
- *D* — treats irrelevance as harmless padding rather than as lost time.

### Q4
A student writes: *"Demand for the good is price inelastic, so the firm should raise its
price and its revenue will increase."* Where does this fall short as an explanation?

- **A. It states the outcome without the linking step: that the proportionate fall in quantity is smaller than the proportionate rise in price, so total revenue rises** ✅
- B. It is wrong — raising price when demand is inelastic reduces revenue
- C. It should have given the numerical value of PED
- D. Nothing is missing; the statement is complete

**Correct: A.** The conclusion is right and the mechanism is absent. This is precisely
the #2 mistake in the corpus — 207 statements — and elasticity is one of its two
biggest hotspots (32 statements).

- *B* — factually incorrect.
- *C* — a number is not what is missing; the *reasoning link* is.
- *D* — **the belief that sustains the error.** Students who pick D cannot see the gap,
  which is why 专题 4 must follow this module for them.

---

### Q5
Which single change would make a good's demand **more** price elastic?

- **A. A close substitute becomes available** ✅
- B. The good takes up a smaller share of consumer income
- C. The time period considered becomes shorter
- D. The good is reclassified as a necessity

**Correct: A.** Substitutability is the dominant determinant.

- *B, C, D* — each moves elasticity the other way. Choosing one indicates the
  determinants are memorised as a list without their direction, which is why elasticity
  reasoning collapses when applied to an unfamiliar context.
