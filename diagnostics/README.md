# Diagnostic quizzes

One quiz per Tier-1/Tier-2 专题 module in `curriculum/module_plan.md`.

## Design rule

**Every distractor is an error an examiner actually reported.** That is the whole
point. A conventional quiz tells a student *how many* they got wrong; these tell them
*which specific misconception they are carrying*, because each wrong option maps to a
named error with a citation.

This is what makes the quiz worth giving an email address for. The output is not
"7/10" — it is "you are substituting value for price when you reason about the terms of
trade, which examiners have flagged every year since 2016, and here is the 8-minute
video that fixes it."

## Format

Each file has, per item: the stem, the options, the correct answer, and a
**diagnosis line** for each wrong option naming the misconception and its evidence.
The diagnosis lines are the deliverable — they drive the result page and the follow-up
sequence.

## Copyright

All items are **original**. No question, stem, or wording is reproduced from any past
paper, mark scheme, or examiner report. Quoted examiner sentences appear only in the
plan and research files as short attributed quotations for analysis. Nothing in the
published quizzes reproduces exam-board material.

## Using these for lead generation

1. Ship the quiz ungated — the student sees their score immediately.
2. Gate the *diagnosis*: the per-item breakdown of which misconception they hold and
   the targeted fix. That is the trade worth making for a contact.
3. Segment the follow-up by which misconception dominated, not by total score. A
   student failing on price-vs-value needs a different first video than one whose
   chains of reasoning are one link short.

## Scoring bands

Bands are deliberately not "grades". They map to a recommended next module:

| Correct | Reading |
|---|---|
| 5/5 | Discrimination is secure; go to the Tier-2 skill modules |
| 3–4/5 | The concept is known but unstable under pressure; watch the module, redo |
| 0–2/5 | The two concepts have merged; this module is the highest-yield thing to fix |
