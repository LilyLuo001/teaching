# Trial lesson design, and the full 知识点 catalogue

How a quiz result turns into a 30-minute trial, what that trial contains, and how
long every one of the 27 知识点 takes to teach properly.

---

## 1. The one design decision everything else follows from

**The trial teaches exactly one 知识点 — the one their quiz says they failed worst.**

Not a syllabus overview, not "tell me about yourself", not a sampler of three
topics. One. Chosen by data they generated themselves ten minutes earlier.

That is the whole differentiator. Every competing free trial opens with "so, what
would you like to work on?" — which puts the diagnosis on the student, who by
definition cannot do it. You open already knowing the answer, and say it in the
first ninety seconds. Nothing else in the lesson buys as much trust as that.

**Corollary: the quiz is not a lead magnet, it is the lesson plan.** If the quiz
is skipped, the trial loses its advantage. Make quiz completion a *requirement*
of booking, not an option — and say why, because the reason is genuinely in the
student's interest.

---

## 2. The 30-minute trial, minute by minute

| Min | Phase | What happens | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | **Name it back** | "You scored 4 out of 8. Five of your errors were the same thing — you're putting the long-run point at minimum average cost. That's what we're fixing today." | Proves the quiz did real work. Converts a score into a named, fixable problem. |
| 2–7 | **Make them do one, out loud** | Give one question on that 考点. They talk through it while you stay quiet. | **The most important five minutes.** The quiz cannot tell a concept gap from a technique gap. A student may know the economics perfectly and still fail Q1 by misreading "not" — that is an E-cluster problem, and teaching them F1 would waste the lesson. You cannot see the difference without hearing them reason. |
| 7–10 | **The reframe** | One sentence that dissolves the misconception. For F1: "The difference isn't the diagram, it's whether a new firm can enter." | Short on purpose. The insight is small; the work is making it stick. |
| 10–20 | **They build it** | *They* draw the diagram, or write the chain of reasoning, while you watch and interrupt. You do not draw. | If you talk for thirty minutes they feel informed and change nothing. Production in the lesson is what makes them book again. This is also where you find the second error they didn't know about. |
| 20–26 | **Real past paper, timed** | One exam question on the same 考点, under time. Then mark it against the real criteria. | Converts "I understand" into "I can do it under pressure", which are different and students routinely confuse them. |
| 26–30 | **The map** | Show what else in their syllabus rests on this 考点, and name the specific next thing. | The honest sales moment. Not "book more lessons" — "this same tangency shows up in F3 and in every long-run efficiency question, so that's the obvious next 45 minutes." |

**Two rules for the trial.**

Set the scope out loud at minute one. If their 知识点 takes 60 minutes and the
trial is 30, say so immediately: *"This topic is four separate mistakes. Today we
fix two — the same two my video covers. The other two are another session."*
Stating the boundary at the start makes it generous. Revealing it at minute 28
makes it bait, and students can tell the difference.

Never end on a cliffhanger. End on something finished, even if small.

## 3. Routing: quiz result → which 知识点

The quiz result names a 考点. The 考点 maps to a 知识点. Two cases:

**Errors concentrated in one 考点** → teach that 知识点. Straightforward.

**Errors spread evenly** → do *not* pick their lowest-scoring topic. A flat
failure across a concept quiz usually means the chain of reasoning is broken, not
the concept — so the highest-yield trial is **D1 (trigger → mechanism → effect)**,
taught using their own topic as the material. Counter-intuitive, and it is the
single most valuable routing rule here: the corpus ranks assertion-without-a-chain
as the #2 mistake overall (207 examiner statements, 54 reports, all five boards),
and it is invisible to a topic quiz.

**They got 8/8** → they are not a trial lead, they are a referral source. Offer
the next tier's quiz instead of a lesson. Selling to them damages credibility.

## 4. The full catalogue — all 27 知识点

### How the durations were produced

These are **estimates from a stated model, not measured teaching times.** I have
not timed these lessons; nobody has. The model is written out so you can
disagree with it and adjust:

```
duration = 20 min base
         + 10 min per 考点 beyond the first
         + 10 min if the student must build a diagram from scratch
         + 10 min if it is a skill unit (needs 3+ practice repetitions)
         +  5 min if it needs a calculation drill
         → rounded to the nearest 30 / 45 / 60
```

The 考点 count comes from the examiner evidence, so that input is real; the
minute weights are judgment. After you have taught five of these, replace my
weights with your own — the model matters more than the numbers.

## 5. Booking more than one 知识点

**Bundle discounts are real, not marketing.** Two 知识点 that share a diagram or a
framework genuinely take less than the sum, because the setup is paid for once.

| Pair | Standalone | Together | Why it's cheaper |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1 + F3** | 105 | **75** | Both live on the same cost curves. Draw AC once. |
| **C1 + C3** | 60 | **45** | Same definition, then examples. C3 is the drill for C1. |
| **C2 + C4** | 90 | **60** | C2 builds the 2×2; C4 is a cell in it. |
| **B1 + B2 + B5** | 120 | **90** | One derivation, then three applications. |
| **A1 + A3** | 60 | **45** | Same index, read two ways. |
| **D1 + D4** | 75 | **60** | D4 is the self-check for the D1 template. |
| **E1 + E2 + E3** | 90 | **60** | One skill — reading the command word — in three costumes. |
| **G1 + G2** | 105 | **90** | Same FRQ, different parts. |

**Prerequisites — do not teach these out of order:**

- **C1 before C2 and C4.** The 2×2 framework is meaningless without the public-good definition.
- **B1 before B2, B3, B5.** The general formula has to exist before you can say one is upside down.
- **D1 before D3.** "Describe vs explain" needs the chain template first.
- **G1 before G2.**
- **A1 before A2 and A4.**

**Cap: two 知识点 per 60 minutes, three per 90.** Past that it is cramming and the
student retains the first one only. Refuse the booking and say why — refusing
money for a stated pedagogical reason is one of the strongest trust signals
available, and it will be remembered.

## 6. Trial → paid, without being pushy

The trial's closing "map" (minute 26–30) should name **one** specific next thing,
priced and scoped, and nothing else. Three options is a sales pitch; one is a
recommendation.

Three natural shapes:

| Package | Contents | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Finish the 知识点** | The remaining 考点 of whatever the trial started | Anyone whose trial topic was a 45- or 60-minute unit. Easiest close — it is literally unfinished. |
| **The cluster** | All 4–5 知识点 in their weakest group, bundled | A student who failed evenly inside one topic |
| **The 14 hours** | All 27, bundled, sequenced by the publishing order | Exam in 3+ months, wants a plan |

**The honest version of the close** is to say which of these they *don't* need. A
student who failed only F1 does not need 14 hours, and telling them so is what
makes them believe you when you later say they do need something.

## 7. Open decisions — yours, not mine

1. **Is the trial free or nominally paid?** Free maximises volume; a small fee
   filters for intent and cuts no-shows sharply. With a quiz gate already
   filtering, free is probably right at launch.
2. **Is quiz completion required to book?** I have assumed **yes** throughout, and
   the design degrades badly without it. If you'd rather not gate, the trial needs
   a different opening five minutes and I should rewrite section 2.
3. **Throughput.** If you are the only teacher, 14 hours per full-package student
   caps how many you can carry. That cap should decide how hard the video CTAs
   push, and it is worth calculating before launch rather than after.
4. **Time zones.** This audience spans China and the UK. Decide your bookable
   window before publishing, not when the first 2am request arrives.
5. **What happens between sessions** for the skill units (B4, B5, D, E, G2). They
   need marked written work to function at all. If you are not marking homework,
   those nine 知识点 should be priced and described differently from the concept
   units — they are a different product.
