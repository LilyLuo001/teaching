# 专题 module plan — built from examiner evidence, not intuition

Every module below is justified by counted statements in official examiner reports.
The counts come from `research/mistake_ranking.xlsx` (sheets 1, 2, 6, 7) and can be
re-derived by filtering the evidence sheet. Citations are board + series so any claim
can be checked against the source report.

**What the counts mean:** the number of separate examiner sentences describing the
error. They are not percentages of students — boards do not publish those. See
`research/README.md`.

## The headline finding that shapes the whole channel

Two mistakes dominate every board and every year:

| | Mistake | Statements | Reports | Boards | Span |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two concepts confused with each other | 292 | 62 | 5 | 2015–2025 |
| 2 | Assertion without explanation / undeveloped chain of reasoning | 207 | 54 | 5 | 2015–2025 |

Third place ("did not answer the question actually set") is 76. So the top two are not
just first, they are in a different weight class.

This has a direct content implication. **Most lost marks are not caused by not knowing
the syllabus.** They are caused by (a) holding two definitions that have blurred into
each other, and (b) stopping an explanation one link early. Both are cross-chapter by
nature, which is exactly the 专题 format — and both are things a 10-minute video can
genuinely fix, unlike "revise harder".

That is also the channel's differentiator: near-100% of competing content re-teaches
the syllabus. Almost none of it attacks the specific discriminations examiners report
failing, year after year.

## Tier 1 — flagship modules

These lead because the evidence is strongest and the error is sharp enough to be
provable to a viewer in the first 30 seconds.

### 专题 1 — Terms of trade is a *price* ratio, not a *value* ratio

**Evidence:** 33 statements across 15 reports, 2015–2025 (CAIE, OCR, Edexcel). 12 of
those explicitly co-mention terms of trade with balance of trade / balance of payments.
This is the single most persistent named confusion in the entire corpus — it recurs in
CAIE reports in 2016, 2022, 2023 and 2025.

**The precise error**, in examiners' own words: candidates *know the formula* and then
discuss the wrong thing.

> "despite knowing the formula for terms of trade, candidates still often confuse the
> concept with balance of payments by then discussing the revenue and expenditure in
> relation to exports and imports" — CAIE 9708, Nov 2023

> "a significant number of candidates still confuse terms of trade with balance of
> trade and, despite knowing the formula, proceed to discuss the value rather than the
> prices of exports and imports" — CAIE 9708, Mar 2025

**Why this is a perfect first video:** the gap is not knowledge, it is a single
substitution (price → value). It is demonstrable, it is worth whole questions, and it
has been costing marks for a decade. Strong hook: *"You can write the formula
correctly and still score zero. Here is how."*

**Cross-chapter span:** international trade · balance of payments · index numbers ·
exchange rates (a depreciation worsens the terms of trade but may improve the trade
balance — the cleanest way to prove the two are different).

**Core teaching move:** one worked case where terms of trade and the balance of trade
move in *opposite* directions. Once a student sees that, the concepts cannot re-merge.

### 专题 2 — The elasticity family: one framework, four elasticities

**Evidence:** 136 statements — the highest-scoring topic in the corpus. Top mistakes
within it: concept confusion (39), assertion without a chain (32), not answering the
question set (11).

**The precise errors:**
- XED written upside down — the percentage change in the price of one good over the
  percentage change in the price of the other, instead of quantity of A over price of B.
  Also "percentage" dropped from the formula entirely (CAIE Jun 2023).
- Knowing the formula but being unable to explain it: candidates "provided the formula
  … and were able to state what represented a relatively elastic and relatively
  inelastic response … but provided confused responses to the explanation"
  (CAIE Nov 2015).
- Writing about *all* the elasticities when the question asked for one — this loses
  time and earns nothing (CAIE Nov 2022, Q2b/Q3b).

**Cross-chapter span:** demand and supply · tax incidence and subsidy incidence ·
firm revenue decisions · protectionism (inelastic PED for imports) · agriculture and
primary product dependence.

**Core teaching move:** teach all four as *one* structure — % change in a response
variable over % change in a driver — so the numerator/denominator is derived rather
than memorised. The upside-down XED error is impossible once the structure is the
thing being recalled.

### 专题 3 — Public vs merit vs demerit goods: a three-way discrimination

**Evidence:** 16 statements co-mentioning merit and public goods across 10 CAIE
reports, 2016–2024. Sits inside "Market failure and externalities", 113 statements, the
#2 topic overall.

**The precise errors:**
- Public-good examples offered for merit goods — "the confusion with public goods
  continued as they gave examples…" (CAIE Jun 2018).
- Vague examples: "some were imprecise and simply offered e.g. 'food' … as a merit
  good" (CAIE Jun 2024). Notably, demerit-good examples were *better* — students find
  the negative case easier, which is a useful teaching entry point.
- Ignoring an instruction in the stem: OCR told candidates flood defences *are* public
  goods; weaker responses ignored that and wrote about positive externalities instead
  (OCR H460, Summer 2023).

**Cross-chapter span:** market failure · government intervention · the role of the
state · information failure.

**Core teaching move:** separate the two axes that students collapse into one.
*Excludability/rivalry* (a property of the good) decides public vs private.
*Information failure / undervaluation* (a property of the consumer) decides merit vs
demerit. Public and merit are answers to different questions, which is why one can
never be an example of the other.

## Tier 2 — the two skill modules

These attack the #1 and #2 mistakes directly, and are topic-agnostic, so they can be
promoted permanently rather than seasonally.

### 专题 4 — From assertion to chain: the three-link explanation

**Evidence:** 207 statements, 54 reports, all 5 boards, 2015–2025. Highest in
Government intervention (32), Elasticities (32), Inflation (17).

The examiner complaint is consistent: the student names a cause and jumps to the
conclusion, skipping the mechanism. AP's Chief Reader reports show the same thing as
"asserting" rather than explaining.

**Core teaching move:** an explicit, checkable template — *trigger → mechanism →
effect on the named variable* — and a habit of asking "why does that follow?" until the
chain reaches the exact term used in the question. Pair with 专题 5, since a chain that
answers a different question still scores nothing.

### 专题 5 — Answer the question actually set

**Evidence:** 76 statements, 28 reports, 4 boards. Concentrated in Inflation (15) and
Government intervention (15).

**The precise errors:** discussing alternative policies when no comparison was asked —
explicitly "irrelevant and will not be rewarded" (CAIE Nov 2022); describing a diagram
instead of explaining it (8 statements); answering a "discuss the features" question by
answering the command word the student expected rather than the one printed.

**Core teaching move:** command-word decoding plus stem-constraint spotting. Rehearse
on stems where the constraint is doing real work — like the OCR flood-defence case
where the stem *hands you* the classification and the marks are for using it.

## Tier 3 — strong topic modules

Ordered by statement count from sheet 6. Each still cross-chapter.

| # | Module | Statements | Dominant mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Government intervention: price controls, taxes, subsidies | 105 | assertion without chain (32) |
| 7 | Inflation: separate cause, measurement and consequence | 86 | assertion without chain (17) |
| 8 | Protectionism and the trade-policy trade-off | 76 | concept confusion (27) |
| 9 | Market structures: the discrimination grid | 68 | concept confusion (24) |
| 10 | Balance of payments vs the government budget | 60 | concept confusion (31) |
| 11 | Labour market: MRP, unions, minimum wage | 60 | concept confusion (16) |
| 12 | Costs and returns: scale vs returns vs cost curves | 56 | concept confusion (18) |

Module 10 is worth flagging: "budget vs current account" appears as a named confusion
pair, and it is a genuine cross-chapter trap because both are called "deficits" while
being unrelated accounts. Module 12 likewise — "economies of scale vs diminishing
returns" and "economies of scale vs returns to scale" both appear as named pairs, and
are a short-run/long-run distinction students routinely lose.

## 专题 13 — AP-specific: the parts that actually score lowest

Unlike the UK boards, College Board publishes real mean scores per FRQ part. This is
the only hard difficulty data in the corpus and deserves its own AP-targeted module.
From sheet 4, the hardest parts sit near **27–30% of available marks**: 2024 Micro Set 2
Q2 (mean 1.33/5), 2025 Macro Set 1 Q2 (1.51/5), 2023 Macro Set 2 Q2 (1.40/5).

For AP the pitch is quantitative and verifiable — *"the average score on this question
was 1.3 out of 5, here is what the Chief Reader said went wrong"* — which is a stronger
hook than anything available for the UK boards.

## Deliberate omissions

Development economics (8 statements) and Macro objectives (1) are too thinly evidenced
to build on. They are omitted rather than padded out. Low counts there reflect thin
examiner coverage, not that students find them easy — see the recall caveats in
`research/README.md`.

## Build order

1. 专题 1 (sharpest provable error, best hook)
2. 专题 2 (largest topic, widest applicability)
3. 专题 4 (skill module, permanent evergreen promotion)
4. 专题 3, then Tier 3 by count
5. 专题 13 as the dedicated AP entry point

Diagnostics for 1–4 are in `diagnostics/`. Each is built so that the wrong answers are
the *documented* errors, which is what makes the result diagnostic rather than just a
score.
