# Shipping list — 2–3 minute 专题 units to pick from

> **Publishing order is in `curriculum/publishing_order.md`.** This file is the full
> menu of 27 units grouped by subject; that file ranks the same 27 by expected audience
> size and says why.


Each row is **one video, one idea, 2–3 minutes** (≈350–450 words of script). The
modules in `module_plan.md` are too big to film as single units, so each is split here
into sub-专题 that can stand alone and be watched in any order.

Every unit carries its evidence: the number of examiner statements behind it, and how
many separate reports they come from. Counts are examiner *mentions*, not student
failure rates — see `research/README.md`.

Pick by number. I'll build the full script, the on-screen beats and the diagram brief
from whichever you choose.

## A — Terms of trade (专题 1)
*Evidence: 33 statements, 15 reports, 2015–2025, CAIE/OCR/Edexcel. The most persistent
named confusion in the whole corpus.*

| # | Unit | The one takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** ⭐ | **Price, not value** | The terms of trade move on *prices* only. Revenue, spending and volumes are irrelevant — and that substitution is the documented error. |
| A2 | **Terms of trade vs balance of trade** | Two different numbers. One worked case where they move in *opposite* directions, which makes re-merging them impossible. |
| A3 | **Reading the index** | What a terms-of-trade index of 96 does and does not tell you. It is not a deficit. |
| A4 | **The depreciation paradox** | A weaker currency *worsens* your terms of trade while possibly *improving* your trade balance. |

## B — The elasticity family (专题 2)
*Evidence: 136 statements — the highest-scoring topic in the corpus.*

| # | Unit | The one takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** ⭐ | **One formula, four elasticities** | Every elasticity is *% change in a response* ÷ *% change in its driver*. Derive it and you can never write one upside down. |
| B2 | **XED upside down** | The specific inversion examiners report (12 statements, 9 reports) — price over price, with no response variable at all. |
| B3 | **Who actually pays the tax?** | Incidence follows the *relative* elasticities of demand and supply, not whether a number is below 1. |
| B4 | **Formula ≠ understanding** | Examiners record students stating the formula correctly and then failing to explain it. What the explanation has to contain. |
| B5 | **Answer only the elasticity asked** | Writing about all four when one was asked earns nothing and costs time. |

## C — Public, merit and demerit goods (专题 3)
*Evidence: 16 co-mentions across 10 CAIE reports, inside the 113-statement market
failure topic.*

| # | Unit | The one takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** ⭐ | **A public good is not "a good the government provides"** | The definition is non-rival + non-excludable — a property of the good, not of its supplier. |
| C2 | **Two axes, not one** | Excludability decides public vs private; information failure decides merit vs demerit. A good sits on *both* at once, which is why neither can be an example of the other. |
| C3 | **Merit good examples that score** | Why "food" earns nothing and "vaccination" earns the mark. |
| C4 | **Demerit good vs negative externality** | A polluting factory is an externality, not a demerit good. Different failure, different fix. |

## D — Building a chain of reasoning (专题 4) — skill
*Evidence: 207 statements, 54 reports, all 5 boards. The #2 mistake overall and it
applies to every topic, so these are evergreen.*

| # | Unit | The one takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** ⭐ | **Trigger → mechanism → effect** | The three-link template, and how to check your own answer against it. |
| D2 | **End on the word in the question** | Stopping at "AD falls" when the question said *inflation* leaves the final mark unearned. |
| D3 | **A diagram is not an explanation** | Describing the diagram instead of explaining it (8 statements, CAIE + AQA). |
| D4 | **The "why does that follow?" test** | A 20-second self-check that finds your own assertions before the examiner does. |

## Recommended first three

**A1 → D1 → C1.**

A1 first: the error is provable in 30 seconds, the evidence runs a decade, and the hook
writes itself — *"you can write the formula correctly and still score zero."*

D1 second: it is the #2 mistake on every board, it is evergreen rather than seasonal,
and it makes every later topic video shorter because you can point back to it.

C1 third: a clean definitional fix with a strong visual, and it feeds the highest-volume
topic area in the corpus.

A1, C1 and D1 already have diagnostics written (`diagnostics/01`, `03`, `04`), so each
can ship with a working lead magnet from day one.
